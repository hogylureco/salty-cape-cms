Capt. Mike’s Notes

If you only fish Buzzards Bay one place, fish the West End. It’s the canal-mouth complex right where the Cape Cod Canal dumps into upper Buzzards Bay. Seven structure types stacked into a four-mile radius: rips off Mashnee Point, deep wrecks in the Hog Island Channel, shallow flats, sheltered coves, the open-water pogy corridor running out of the canal mouth. When the rest of the bay is blown to hell on a SW afternoon or post-frontal NW, the West End still fishes. That’s why it’s the spot.

What it’s known for is the spring trophy bass. The first big keeper of the season comes out of here every year. Pogies start pushing into the canal mouth in late May, and the bass come with them. Then it switches gears , May opener loads up the Hog Island wrecks with sea bass and scup, July through August is fluke on the Mashnee Rip, September brings the albies and the migration bass. Six months of active fishery, seven target species, five different game plans. Nothing else in Buzzards covers that range.

Location & Overview

The West End of the Canal is a wide area in Upper Buzzards Bay that includes Dry Ledge, Mashnee Flats, and the open water surrounding the entrance channel to the Cape Cod Canal. The West End extends from the Massachusetts Maritime Academy out to the end of Wings Neck and Stoney Point Dike. No fishing is allowed east of the MMA per Army Corps regulation, and they actively patrol this area and will issue citations. While Buzzards Bay is often associated with rough fishing conditions, particularly in the afternoon, this area is great in a pinch. You can put in nearby and stay relatively protected when afternoon chop builds on the outer bay.

The Canal is the trigger for the entire West End system. When bait starts dumping out of the Canal, fish can show from the Hog Island edge to Mashnee and Pocasset. But a strong southwest wind against Canal current can make the Hog Island crossing rough fast. When conditions deteriorate, stay inside the system and fish protected water. The fish often move less than you think. Monument, Pocasset, Mashnee lee shores, docks, flats, and cove mouths all become viable options.

The West End is a top spot in the early spring and again in the fall. The water is shallow and sandy, so it warms quickly and becomes home to serious boat traffic in peak season. Each year, the first big keeper stripers of the season seem to come from here, including Jeff Fortin’s 62 lb monster, a fish he tried for over an hour to revive. Those who know Jeff know this guy tried harder than almost anyone you can think of. The West End is also home to great early-season groundfish. A mixed bag of monster scup, sea bass, and tog stack on the wrecks and ledges starting with the May opener.

What makes the West End distinctive is seven structure types packed into a 4-mile radius, all accessible from Taylor Point, all fishable in conditions that shut down most of the outer bay. Rips at Mashnee Island Point. Open-water pogy corridor through Canal mouth and Hog Island Channel. Shallow boulder fields on the point-and-rip system. Deep boulders and wrecks at Hog Island Channel. Deep channels at Canal mouth. Shallow flats at Mashnee. Estuaries and coves at Pocasset and Monument Beach. Five tactical approaches and seven target species inside one compact complex.

  • Seven structure types in a 4-mile complex. More method variety than almost any other inshore fishery in the library. Rips, points, open water, shallow boulders, deep wrecks, deep channels, flats, and estuaries.
  • Five tactical approaches (B.C1 Spring Birds / B.C2 Mashnee Swing / G.J1 Sea Bass / G.J2 Fluke / B.T Channel Trolling). The only Featured Spot in the library with a dedicated fluke program.
  • Sheltered geography makes this the reliable Buzzards Bay weather-fallback fishery. When the outer bay is blown out, West End still fishes.
  • Seven target species, striped bass, bluefish, false albacore, black sea bass, scup, fluke, tautog, broadest species mix in the library.

All of this means a full day here can flow across every kind of fishing. Dawn bird plays at Mass Maritime, rip swings at Mashnee Point through morning tide, sea bass jigging at Hog Island during slack, fluke drifts across Mashnee Rip through midday, channel trolling on the afternoon tide change. Five approaches, seven structure types, seven species, six months of active fishery. The canal-mouth complex that’s earned its reputation as Upper Buzzards Bay’s most versatile spot.

Structure & Approach, West End

What this spot is most known for.

The West End is a seven-structure compound concentrated in a 4-mile complex at the western Canal mouth. Rips form off Mashnee Island Point where tide rakes across shallow boulder fields. Open water with pogy schools runs the migration corridor between the Canal mouth and Hog Island. Shallow boulder fields line the Mashnee shoreline and outer Canal jetty. Deep boulders and wrecks stack in Hog Island Channel at 30–65 ft. Deep channels funnel Canal outflow through the jetty cut. Shallow flats at Mashnee warm first in spring and hold schoolies and fluke into summer. Estuaries and shallow coves at Pocasset and Monument Beach serve as early-season nurseries and fall kayak water. Seven structure types, five tactical approaches, seven species.

The spot is known for three things.

  • The spring trophy window. First big keeper stripers of the season on pogies pushing into the Canal mouth. Early May through mid-June; often the first 40+ lb fish on the Cape every year.
  • The May sea bass opener. Hog Island Channel wrecks and deep boulder fields stack heavy with sea bass, scup mixed in. Textbook light-jig groundfishing on the opener.
  • Summer fluke program. The only dedicated fluke approach in the Salty Cape library. Mashnee Rip drifts where tide pulls bait off the flat and funnels it across gravel and rubble ambush zones.

What Makes This Spot Fishy, West End

Canal-Driven Current Dynamics

The West End benefits from swift tidal currents funneled through the Cape Cod Canal into Buzzards Bay. This movement creates an ideal situation for baitfish, pogies, sand eels, silversides, to gather in high-density schools, attracting predators. The rip lines formed by these currents are prime feeding grounds for striped bass, bluefish, and false albacore. The rips provide both surface action and deeper feeding zones, offering options at various depths depending on the tide.

Structure Variety Unmatched in Buzzards Bay

Seven structure types compressed into a 4-mile complex. Shallow rip zones for surface feeders like bluefish. Deep wrecks and channels at Hog Island for sea bass and fluke. Mashnee Flats as spring nursery water. Pocasset coves as sheltered backwater. This variety lets you fish multiple approaches in one session. Cast to breaking fish in the morning, drift rocky bottoms for fluke midday, jig deep channels for sea bass into afternoon.

Method Flexibility

The diversity of structures, current flows, and tides supports an array of fishing methods. Anglers can drift or spot-lock over wrecks for sea bass and scup. Use topwater plugs in rip lines for bluefish. Jig deep channels for sea bass and tautog. Slow-troll tubes along channel contours for bass. Drift fluke rigs across the Mashnee Rip. Whether casting, drifting, or trolling, this area covers every inshore fishing preference.

Tidal Funnel / Pinch Point

The West End acts as a natural funnel or pinch point where fish and bait are funneled from Buzzards Bay into the Canal and vice versa. This makes it a prime location for migratory fish using this corridor as a passageway. During the flood, water pushes baitfish up and over the structure, attracting feeding predators. The ebb creates fast-moving water that pushes fish toward the edges of the rips, where anglers target with jigs or swimmers.

Bait Reliability Across Six Months

Baitfish presence is consistent. Pogy schools are prevalent during spring and fall migrations. Silversides and sand eels dominate summer months. Fish stage at the Canal mouth feeding on abundant bait pushing through. Steady bait availability makes this a reliable fishery year-round, especially during seasonal runs. Bait diversity drives the bite. Anglers match the available forage whether squid, sand eels, pogy, or crab.

The “Sheltered Weather-Fallback” Signature

When Buzzards Bay’s outer water is blown out, afternoon SW chop, post-frontal NW gales, SE swell through the mid-bay, West End still fishes. The geography provides shelter; Wings Neck and Stoney Point cut the worst of the NW; Mashnee provides lee from SW afternoon chop; Pocasset and Monument coves are inside water regardless of what the bay does. This is what makes it the reliable Buzzards Bay weather-fallback fishery. When other spots are washed out, you can still put in at Taylor Point and find productive water.

Boat Pressure Notes

This area can get crowded, especially during peak spring and fall seasons, and boat traffic has a big impact on fish behavior. Early mornings, especially on outgoing tides, are prime for a solid bite. As more boats arrive and motor around, fish become very wary and the bite slows significantly. I’ve seen fish feeding actively one moment, then vanish the next once engine noise and boat wake increase.

  • Pressure concentrates on named features. Mass Maritime Point outflow, Mashnee Point rip, Hog Island Channel wrecks. Weekend mornings in peak seasons (May–June trophy bass; September fall run) can be particularly stacked.
  • Transit traffic is heavy and constant. Commercial, ferry, and recreational traffic transits the Canal mouth regardless of fishing pressure. Courtesy and spatial awareness non-negotiable. Stay wide of main transit lanes; let commercial boats pass clean.
  • The pressure-management play is rotation. When Mashnee Point is stacked, slide to Hog Island Channel or Mashnee Flats. When Hog Island has a fleet, shift to Mass Maritime outflow or the Open Water Flats. The 6-sub-spot loop provides natural rotation options that absorb peak-season pressure gracefully.

The best approach: get in early and keep your approach quiet. The quieter and more calculated your drift, the better the chances of success. If the area gets busy, try to position just outside the immediate feeding zone. You can often pick up fish on the outskirts of the action by casting jigs or soft plastics where bait moves in and out of the rip, but be mindful of pressure on the area. One clean drift outproduces five noisy passes every time.

Seasonal Timing & Bait Presence

May

  • Bait: Sand eels, silversides, first pogy schools arriving. Herring still pouring out of Canal. Squid starting on Mashnee Point.
  • Target: First migratory stripers stage at Mass Maritime Point and Hog Island Channel on silversides and herring. Bluefish starting. Black sea bass opener on wrecks.
  • Tactics: Charter Grade Poppers and Dog Walkers in morning. Protail Paddle mid-column. Squinnow jigs on wrecks. Spring trophy window primary; first big keepers every year come from here.

June

  • Bait: Pogy schools thick at Canal mouth drifting into Buzzards Bay. Sand eels and silversides continue. Squid on Mashnee through dusk.
  • Target: Peak spring bass. Keepers and cow bass stage below pogy schools. Bluefish increasing. Fluke arriving on Mashnee Rip shallow edges.
  • Tactics: In-Line Epoxy Jig + Protail Paddle under pogy schools. CGDW - Hogy Charter Grade Dog Walker (8.25", 3.5oz) on Mashnee swing. Sea bass and scup on Hog Island wrecks. First fluke drifts across Mashnee Rip.

July

  • Bait: Sand eels dominate. Silversides thick. Juvenile squid in pockets. Pogies thinning but still present.
  • Target: Bluefish dominating surface action. Stripers staging on structure edges. Peak fluke in 15–25 ft rip lanes. Sea bass on wrecks.
  • Tactics: Mashnee Point rip casting peak on outgoing. Fluke drifts primary midday. Channel trolling on afternoon tide change. Albies not yet reliable.

August

  • Bait: Sand eels continue; silversides dominant; juvenile squid and butterfish mixed. Pogies sporadic.
  • Target: Bluefish dominant mid-morning through afternoon. Albies starting late month on bait balls. Sea bass scattered deeper. Fluke still productive.
  • Tactics: Deep trolling for bass under bluefish packs. Surface plugs through rips for blues. First albie sessions late month on Open Water Flats.

September

  • Bait: Peanut bunker and anchovies arriving. Sand eels thick. Silversides continue. Small squid still present.
  • Target: Fall bass returning on peanut migrations. Albies consistent across all sub-spots. Bluefish thinning. Tautog starting on shallow rock.
  • Tactics: Full 5-approach rotation peak month. Albie sessions on Flats and Open Water. Bass return on incoming tide into rips. Fluke migrating to deeper water (25–35 ft base of rip).

October

  • Bait: Peanut bunker dominant; sand eels continue. Adult bunker and mackerel push through.
  • Target: Bass strong on peanut migrations. Albies persist until first hard blow. Sea bass in deeper wrecks. Tautog peak on crab-rich boulders.
  • Tactics: Topwater bass on incoming. Surface Eraser for albie frenzies. Crab-tipped jigs for tog. Tube trolling productive all day with cooler water.

Outgoing Tide (Primary Program)

The outgoing tide is often the most productive time to fish this area. As water flows out of the Canal and into Buzzards Bay, the current picks up speed, creating strong rips and currents that concentrate baitfish. This movement brings schools of bait like pogy, sand eels, and silversides toward the deeper areas and rip lines, which are prime feeding grounds for striped bass, bluefish, and false albacore. Fish are generally positioned near structure, boulders, wrecks, and the rip lines, waiting for bait to flush through. Striped bass and bluefish, in particular, will be tight to the current seam, ambushing bait that gets swept by the flow.

Incoming Tide (Secondary Program)

The incoming tide pushes water into the Canal, slowing down current speeds and creating different feeding conditions. Fish can often be found near the edges of the rip lines, waiting for bait to be pushed in with the current. This is also the time when baitfish start to move into the Canal from Buzzards Bay, attracting predators. Fish tend to move closer to the shallower areas as the tide pushes water in, particularly around structure like rocks, wrecks, and drop-offs. Bluefish and stripers may hold tight to structure, while false albacore and bonito tend to move into deeper water. As the tide changes direction, reposition frequently to stay on top of fish. Drift edges of structure or move into deeper channel areas for more success.

Slack Tide

Slack tide, when water transitions from outgoing to incoming (or vice versa), is often a dead zone. Fish tend to become less active during this period as current slows or stops. This is not to say fishing stops completely. Certain species, like tautog and sea bass, may still bite in deeper water around structure. The overall action tends to be slower, so use slack to reposition, reset drifts, or switch approaches. Hog Island Channel stays workable throughout slack for groundfish; surface programs collapse and rebuild on next cycle.

Optimal Winds

Southwest: The reference summer wind. Aligns with the outgoing tide, helping to push the current in a more favorable direction. This setup helps stack up the rips and create clean feeding zones for fish. The wind smooths out the surface chop, making for more comfortable conditions and clearer visibility. Ideal for casting to the rip lines; swimbaits and soft plastics work well along seams and rips; vertical jigging around wrecks and boulders highly productive with smooth surface allowing subtle bite feel.

Northwest: Flattens the water out, making for a much smoother surface and clearer conditions. Keeps water temperature in check, especially during summer, which can result in fish being more active, especially during early morning and late evening. Works well for both casting and jigging. Allows for easier control of presentation, especially on rips and structure edges. Swimbaits and soft plastics work particularly well when you need to slow retrieves to entice more lethargic fish feeding along bottom or edges.

Northeast: Not a classic optimal wind for the complex as a whole, but because of the West End’s sheltered geography, NE workable on western sub-spots. Pocasset coves, Monument Beach, and western Hog Island Channel edge. Rotate east-to-west on NE days; Mashnee Point and outer flats cross-chopped.

Problematic Winds

East: Disrupts the tide’s natural flow and creates chop, especially in Buzzards Bay. This can make for a more uncomfortable and challenging drift. The current will shift unpredictably, making it more difficult to stay on top of the fish. Water clarity can also drop with increased wave action. Swimbaits and other light lures may not be as effective, as the chop creates a more erratic drift, which can interfere with accurate presentations.

Southeast: Creates challenging conditions, as it can cause chop and swell, especially close to the shoreline or in the Canal’s narrower sections. The wind pushes against the natural current flow, which can make fishing near the rips difficult as the water becomes more unsettled. Casting, especially topwater and swimbait presentations, may be less effective due to the choppy water and the difficulty of controlling the bait’s movement.

Post-Storm Swell: After a storm, the waters can remain stirred up, causing residual swells that disrupt the fishing conditions even after the wind has died down. Presentations degraded; drifts sloppy. Hog Island Channel deep structure recovers fastest; shallow Mashnee Flats and Pocasset coves need 24–48 hours for clarity to return.

Weather Considerations, West End

Rain and Overcast Extend Low-Light Windows

Rain and overcast are reliable friends at the West End. Cloud cover extends the low-light window for bird plays at Mass Maritime Point and holds stripers tighter to the rip at Mashnee Point past sunrise. Topwater plugs stay effective well past first light on drizzly mornings. Light rain masks boat presence and softens surface tension, making presentations more natural across open water and shallow flats.

Fog Common in Spring (May–Jun)

Expect limited visibility on early morning runs. Canal traffic is heavy and constant regardless of visibility. Radar discipline non-negotiable when crossing from any Taylor Point or Monument Beach launch. Commercial and ferry traffic transits the Canal mouth; draggers work the outer bay. Non-locals should stage well off the Canal entrance until visibility improves.

Summer SW Sea Breeze

Consistent SW sea breeze builds from late morning through afternoon in summer. Dawn sessions that start well can deteriorate by 10–11 AM as breeze stacks on the outer Bay. Tube trolling and deep jigging handle afternoon breeze better than surface programs. Plan peak-window casting sessions for dawn; shift to channel trolling or deep structure by midday.

Post-Storm Reset Windows

Post-storm clearing windows produce some of the best runs of the year. Bait distribution reshuffles; compression rebuilds; fish feed aggressively as stability returns. First stable day after a system often produces the best bite of the week, especially for the spring trophy bass window and September fall run.

Onshore Wind Activates Shallow Programs

Onshore winds (SW for Mashnee Flats, NE for Pocasset coves) push bait into shallow structure and stack whitewater on boulder fields. Poppers and walkers directly into wash are the primary presentation. Offshore winds flatten water, disperse bait, and make fish spooky. Switch to subtle swimmers and soft plastics with extended casts. The complex has an onshore direction for most sub-spots, which is part of why it fishes well in varied conditions.

Salty System Part 3: The B.A.S.E.™ Layer, Observational Factors

Observation Factors (B.A.S.E.)

B: Birds & Bait

The B.A.S.E. framework, Birds, Activity, Structure, Echoes, helps fine-tune your approach. Here’s how to read the water based on local conditions at the West End.

Birds

  • Terns diving or dipping: Targeting small bait such as sand eels or silversides. Focus on rip lines near Mass Maritime Point or Stony Point where small schools active. Ideal for small poppers or soft plastics like paddletails.
  • Gulls hovering / circling: Usually targeting larger bait like pogies or menhaden. Good sign larger bait is pushing through rips near Hog Island Channel or Mashnee Island Point. Cast swimbaits or topwater plugs to catch predators lurking below.
  • Birds over structure (rips / channel mouths): Fish likely feeding in those areas. Keep an eye on tide and wind direction to predict best spots, especially near Mashnee Flats or Hog Island Channel.
  • No birds, calm morning: Pre-feed or post-feed window. Sight-cast visible cruisers; scan sonar for staged fish; don’t force bird play.

Bait (standalone tells, no birds required)

  • Silversides dimpling surface at dawn: Pre-feed tension at Mass Maritime Point. Light topwater or small In-Line Epoxy.
  • Pogy schools visible near Canal mouth: Spring trophy window signal. CGDW - Hogy Charter Grade Dog Walker (8.25", 3.5oz) or large Protail Paddle; fish under pogies for bigger bass.
  • Bait dimpling across Mashnee Flats: Flood-tide bait push. Small soft plastics or clousers; long casts to tailing fish.
  • Bait balls offshore with albie splashes: Late-August / September albie signal. In-Line Epoxy Jig R401 - Surface Burn; fire once and reset.

A: Activity

  • Random surface crashes: Bait likely scattered. Fast erratic retrieves like swimbaits or fast-moving plugs to match feeding near Hog Island Channel or Mashnee Flats.
  • Multiple boils: Fish pushing bait ball beneath surface. Drop countdown jig or sub-surface soft plastic to catch fish under surface, especially along Mass Maritime Point.
  • Visible cruisers: Stripers slowly cruising. Slow-rolled soft plastics or swimbait to entice them. Cast ahead of fish and retrieve at steady pace, especially in deeper areas around Hog Island Channel.
  • Explosive slashes with bait spraying: Blues or albies in active feeding mode. Metal jigs or poppers. Burn through the feed at Mashnee Island Point.
  • Stripers tailing on Mashnee Flats (windless nights): Summer nighttime tell. Long casts; small soft plastics; precision over volume.
  • Pogies being crashed from below: Trophy bass ambush. CGDW - Hogy Charter Grade Dog Walker (8.25", 3.5oz) or large Protail Paddle right in the mix.

S: Structure

  • Rip lines at Mashnee Point + Canal mouth: Constant at West End due to Canal current. Fish stage at seams waiting for bait pushed through. Cast topwater like Charter Grade Poppers into breaking water near Hog Island Channel or Stony Point.
  • Channel edges (Hog Island): Fish rest as they wait for bait washed through. Sub-surface soft plastics or countdown jigs when fish holding along deeper sections.
  • Shallow boulder fields (Mashnee Point): Boulders provide great structure. Work drop-offs around boulders where bass and bluefish hold. Weedless rigs or jigs to prevent snags.
  • Flats at Mashnee (calm mornings / evenings): Fish move from deeper areas to shallower zones. Cast soft plastics into rip, especially during slack tide transition, for great results.
  • Canal outflow seam (Mass Maritime): Fast-moving seam where Canal outflow meets Bay water. Drift weighted soft plastics or live eels down the seam; stripers and blues ambush bait sweeping past.
  • Estuary coves (Pocasset / Monument): Sheltered backwater structure. Kayak-accessible; small soft plastics near rocks and docks during outgoing tides.

E: Echoes (Sonar)

  • Stacked arches on wrecks: Active feeding fish, most likely sea bass or striped bass on bottom. Drop heavy jig or bucktail to match position near wrecks or deep edges of Hog Island Channel.
  • Half arches or flat bands tight to bottom: Scup or fluke grubbing along wreck edges. Drop sand eel jig or small jig to catch them in these areas.
  • Diagonally streaking bait clouds: Albies or bluefish moving fast targeting baitfish like menhaden or sand eels. Throw metal jig or deep-diving swimming plug to match their pace, especially near Mashnee Flats in fall.
  • Isolated larger arches: Often represent larger sea bass or tautog, especially if hovering above bottom. Drop rig just above bottom and retrieve slowly near boulder fields or wrecks.
  • Crescent marks on channel contour: Staged stripers on tube trolling line. Tube program indicator for B.T approach.

Q&A with Capt Mike

Spot facts

Where is the West End of the Canal in Buzzards Bay?

The West End of the Canal is the canal-mouth complex in upper Buzzards Bay, right where the Cape Cod Canal empties into the bay near Bourne. It combines swift tidal current, shallow flats, deep channels, and point-rip structure, centered at 41.7032, -70.6310.

What are the coordinates for the West End of the Canal?

The West End complex centers at 41.7032, -70.6310 in decimal degrees, at the western mouth of the Cape Cod Canal in upper Buzzards Bay. It runs from the canal outflow and Hog Island Channel to Mashnee Point and the surrounding flats.

Why is the West End of the Canal such a good fishing spot?

The West End is upper Buzzards Bay’s most versatile spot because the Cape Cod Canal acts as a conveyor belt, dumping bait and swift current into the bay. That flow meets shallow flats, deep channels, and classic point-rip geometry, so Striped Bass and Bluefish stack on the current seams while bottom species hold on the channel structure. If you fish Buzzards Bay one place, this is the spot.

What fish can you catch at the West End of the Canal?

The West End of the Canal holds Striped Bass and Bluefish on the current seams and point rips through the season, with Black Sea Bass on the channel structure in late spring and Fluke on the sand edges through summer.

When is the best time to fish the West End of the Canal?

The West End fishes best in early spring and again in fall for Striped Bass and Bluefish, when bait pours through the canal. Black Sea Bass hold on the channel structure in late spring, and Fluke work the sand edges through summer. The shallow, sandy water warms quickly, which kicks off the early-season bite.

What tide is best at the West End of the Canal?

Everything keys on the canal flow, so the West End fires on a running tide (when the current is actively flowing, between high and low rather than at slack). The moving water concentrates bait through the west end and along the point rips, and the bass and blues stack on the seams where the canal current meets the bay. Slack water shuts the bite down.

How deep is the water at the West End of the Canal?

The West End ranges from about 3 to 65 feet. Shallow flats and coves run 3 to 15 feet, the point rips sit in 10 to 25 feet, and the canal channel and outflow drop to 30 to 65 feet.

What is the best lure for striped bass at the West End of the Canal?

When sand eels and silversides push through the outflow, a Hogy In-Line Epoxy Jig around 7/8 ounce takes Striped Bass and Bluefish on the seams. When larger pogies or menhaden are pushing, a Hogy Charter Grade Dog Walker 8.25 inch and 3.5 ounce in Glow or Amber is the trophy topwater call, and a Hogy Protail Paddle 5.5 inch in Olive covers fish holding subsurface.

What is the nearest boat ramp to the West End of the Canal?

Taylor Point Ramp in Bourne (41.7427, -70.6155) is the primary launch, about 1.2 nautical miles northeast, with Monument Beach Ramp (41.7136, -70.6168) about 0.7 nautical miles southeast as a close alternate. The West End is a boat fishery.

What are the hazards at the West End of the Canal?

The West End is shallow and sandy with very swift canal current, so strong flow and point rips demand careful boat handling, especially where the canal meets the bay. The water warms fast and draws heavy boat traffic in peak season, and Cape Cod Canal vessel traffic runs just to the east. Mind the shallow flats and shifting sand.

Is the West End of the Canal or Cleveland Ledge better for fishing?

Both are upper Buzzards Bay spots but fish for different things. The West End of the Canal is a current-driven canal-mouth complex where Striped Bass and Bluefish stack on point rips and seams, best in spring and fall. Cleveland Ledge, about 6.5 nautical miles south-southwest, is the bay’s deep rock-and-wreck groundfish complex for sea bass, scup, tog, and fluke. Fish the West End for canal-current bass and Cleveland Ledge for bottom species.

What are the best fishing spots in upper Buzzards Bay near the Canal?

Near the head of upper Buzzards Bay, the West End of the Canal is the marquee canal-mouth complex for Striped Bass and Bluefish. Wings Neck lies about 3.2 nautical miles south-southwest, Cleveland Ledge about 6.5 nautical miles south-southwest is the groundfish anchor, and Bird Island and the Marion and Mattapoisett shore sit about 4.9 nautical miles southwest. Each runs its own seasonal window, so the best choice tracks the tide, bait, and time of year.
